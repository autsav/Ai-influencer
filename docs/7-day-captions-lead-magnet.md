**Scene:** Busy cafe, laptop open, not looking at the screen — looking past it, thinking

So we download apps. We buy planners. We spend a Sunday building Notion dashboards.

And six weeks later, it's all abandoned.

The truth is messier. Systems only stick when they're boring enough to maintain. The fancy workflows people post online? Most of them fall apart by week three.

The ones that actually work are the ones you stop noticing.

## Saturday — The Automation First Step
**Scene:** Whiteboard, bold marker, workflow diagram sketched out, casual but focused energy

If I could give every new founder one piece of advice, it would be this:

Don't try to automate everything at once.

Pick the most annoying recurring task you have — the one that shows up every week and drains you just thinking about it.

That's your first automation. Everything else comes after.

For me it was email triage. Four hours a week, gone. Now it's forty minutes, handled by a simple ruleset that runs while I sleep.

## Sunday — The Evening Reset
**Scene:** Cozy evening, warm lamp, journal open, no laptop in sight, peaceful energy

Sunday evenings are for closing loops.

Not planning the week — that's different. Planning is active. This is more like... taking inventory. What worked. What didn't. Where I'm carrying tension I don't need.

Most founders skip this part because it feels indulgent. It isn't.

The weeks I've checked in with myself on Sunday are the ones where I show up sharper on Monday. Consistently.

It's not a productivity ritual. It's a maintenance one.
